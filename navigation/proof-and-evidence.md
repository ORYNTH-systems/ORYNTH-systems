# ORYNTH Proof and Evidence

ORYNTH treats evidence as part of execution integrity—not as an explanation added after an action has already occurred.

A technically successful output does not, by itself, prove that the action was authorized, admissible, continuous, correctly executed, accurately observed, reconciled, or final.

> **Evidence can support a determination. Evidence cannot create the authority being evaluated.**

## What ORYNTH means by proof

Within ORYNTH, a proof is a bounded and inspectable demonstration connecting declared inputs, governing conditions, architectural controls, decisions, outputs, and preserved evidence.

A complete proof should identify:

1. the architecture and version evaluated;
2. the initial state and proposed action;
3. the asserted authority source;
4. the applicable constraints and invariants;
5. the constitutional decision boundary;
6. the admitted, denied, blocked, or discontinuous outcome;
7. the reason for that outcome;
8. the evidence preserved for reconstruction;
9. the reproducibility conditions;
10. the limits of the resulting conclusion.

Proof is not synonymous with successful execution. A deterministic refusal, preserved denial reason, or verified discontinuity may be the correct proof outcome.

## Governing evidence distinctions

| Distinction | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Output is not evidence of authority | A valid-looking result may be generated without authority to effectuate it |
| Evidence is not authority | Records support evaluation but cannot authorize the action under examination |
| Authorization is not continuity | Earlier permission may become invalid following material state change |
| Execution is not verified effect | An attempted action does not prove the intended consequence occurred |
| Effect is not resolution | A produced result does not establish that the underlying condition is resolved |
| Resolution is not reconciliation | Intended, executed, observed, and evidentiary states must still align |
| Reconstruction is not restoration | Rebuilding a record does not prove that original continuity was preserved |
| Replay is not execution | Historical behavior must be inspectable without reproducing external effects |
| Passing cases are not universal proof | A bounded corpus supports conclusions only within its declared scope |
| Absence of observed failure is not absence of defects | Untested or undiscovered conditions may remain outside the corpus |

## Evidence lifecycle

| Stage | Governing question |
|---|---|
| Request | What action was proposed, by whom, and against what target? |
| Authority | What principal, role, delegation, scope, and jurisdiction were asserted? |
| Admissibility | Was the request eligible to enter constitutional evaluation? |
| Continuity | Did authority, policy, identity, dependency, time, and system state remain valid? |
| Admission | Did an independent boundary admit or deny effectuation? |
| Execution | What operation was actually attempted? |
| Effect | What consequential state change was independently observed? |
| Evidence | Which records, reasons, receipts, hashes, and transitions were preserved? |
| Resolution | Was the condition resolved, denied, compensated, reversed, or recovered? |
| Reconciliation | Do intended, executed, observed, evidentiary, and consequential states agree? |
| Finality | Did the transition reach its declared terminal state under applicable rules? |
| Reconstruction | Can the decision be inspected without inventing authority or reproducing effects? |

No stage automatically proves the next stage.

## Public proof surfaces

### Unified Agency Architecture Execution Prevention Lab

The [UAA Execution Prevention Lab](https://github.com/ORYNTH-systems/uaa-execution-prevention-lab) evaluates whether actions lacking sufficient authority, delegation, scope, admissibility, or execution-boundary approval are prevented.

Its declared public corpus reports 100 cases, 100 prevented failures, and zero observed unauthorized executions within the declared corpus.

It addresses:

- separation of capability from authority;
- bounded principal-agent relationships;
- scope and delegation enforcement;
- deterministic denial;
- replay resistance;
- preservation of denial evidence.

[UAA Execution Prevention Lab software record](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20820868)

### Execution Integrity Protocol

The [EIP Protocol validation corpus](https://github.com/ORYNTH-systems/eip-protocol) evaluates continuity between an earlier authorization state and the state present immediately before effectuation.

It addresses:

- rule-identity continuity;
- state-divergence detection;
- temporal validity;
- dependency continuity;
- stale-authorization rejection;
- deterministic continuity denial;
- preservation of divergence evidence.

[Foundational EIP record](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20127973) | [EIP Reference Corpus](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20818943)

### Proof of Block

## How to evaluate a public proof surface

A reviewer should:

1. identify the repository, release, branch, commit, version, or DOI;
2. read the declared scope and exclusions;
3. locate the case or corpus manifest;
4. identify the expected result for each case;
5. follow the repository’s declared execution or inspection instructions;
6. preserve environment and dependency information;
7. compare observed results with expected results;
8. examine reasons—not merely pass or failure counts;
9. verify that replay and reconstruction remain non-effectuating;
10. record deviations, missing evidence, and unresolved assumptions;
11. distinguish demonstrated behavior from untested claims;
12. cite the exact artifact and version reviewed.

If a repository lacks sufficient execution instructions, manifests, expected-result records, or environment declarations, that absence is an evidence limitation and must not be silently inferred away.

---

## Minimum reproducibility record

A reproducibility record should contain:

- repository and artifact name;
- commit, release, version, or DOI;
- execution date;
- operating environment;
- dependencies and versions;
- configuration;
- case identifier;
- initial state;
- proposed action;
- applicable authority and constraints;
- expected outcome;
- observed outcome;
- preserved logs or receipts;
- relevant hashes;
- deviations;
- reviewer identity;
- unresolved limitations.

## What public evidence can establish

Public evidence can support bounded conclusions about whether declared ORYNTH mechanisms behaved as specified within particular corpora, versions, assumptions, implementations, and test environments.

It may provide evidence of:

- deterministic admission or denial;
- preserved authority boundaries;
- detected discontinuity;
- constraint enforcement;
- provenance preservation;
- verification admissibility;
- traceable findings;
- reproducible outcomes within a declared corpus.

## What public evidence does not establish

Public availability or successful bounded testing does not independently establish:

- universal correctness;
- complete coverage of every possible failure state;
- production readiness;
- production security;
- legal or regulatory compliance;
- governmental approval;
- certification or accreditation;
- suitability for a particular deployment;
- permanent prevention of every possible failure;
- absence of undiscovered defects.
